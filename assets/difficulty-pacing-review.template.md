## Genre questions

- Could the player understand why the current beat was difficult and what skill would improve the result?
- Did novelty arrive only after its prerequisite was learned, then reappear in changed contexts and combinations?
- Did peaks feel earned and readable? Did relief restore attention without becoming dead time?
- Were failures attributable, recovery inviting, and retries short enough for the promised experience?
- Which difficulty dimensions were felt simultaneously? Did workload exceed readable/perceptual limits?
- Did any number inflation, grind, obscurity, backtracking, input friction, randomness or content repetition masquerade as difficulty?
- For puzzle: were base concepts combined and transfer tested without hidden rules?
- For action/horror/co-op: did normal play show real peaks and valleys rather than permanent maximum intensity?
- For RPG/progression/idle: did scaling preserve the meaning of earned power and player choices?
- For racing: was catch-up fair, bounded and non-exploitable?
- For extraction/open world/sandbox: could the player select risk and recover from loss?
- For competitive multiplayer: did matchmaking, side/team composition and uncertainty—not hidden mid-match DDA—carry adaptation?
- For narrative: were comprehension and decision load paced around revelations and branches?
